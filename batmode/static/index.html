A LITTLE PHYSICS. A LITTLE WONDER.

Your laptop is
listening to motion.

The speakers emit a tone near 19 kHz. When sound bounces off your moving hand, its frequency shifts—the same Doppler effect that changes the pitch of a passing siren.

01

Emit. A quiet high-frequency tone travels from the speakers.

02

Listen. The microphone measures the original tone and shifted reflections.

03

Move time. Higher frequencies advance the scene. Lower frequencies rewind it.

This senses motion, not a hand's identity or exact location. Nearby movement can also register. A stationary hand should leave time frozen.

Python handles the microphone locally. This page never requests your camera or microphone, uploads audio, or records it to disk. Stop sonar or close the page to release the microphone.
